Privacy Policy

GallerySync — last updated 18 September 2026

The short version. GallerySync sends your photos and videos to your own Microsoft OneDrive account and nowhere else. The developer operates no server, receives none of your data, and cannot see your photos, your file names, or your account. There is no analytics, no advertising, and no tracking of any kind. GallerySync can also change files on your phone and, if you allow it, in your OneDrive — but only in the ways you choose, and never by permanently deleting anything. See exactly what it can change.

What the app does with your data

GallerySync backs up photos and videos from your device to your own OneDrive account. To do that it needs to read the media on your device and to sign in to your Microsoft account.

Depending on the choices you make, it can also free up space on your phone once a file is safely in OneDrive, and bring full-size originals back from OneDrive to your phone. Each of these is described under What GallerySync can change below.

Your photos travel from your device directly to Microsoft's OneDrive service over an encrypted connection. They do not pass through any server belonging to the developer, because no such server exists. Files are uploaded exactly as they are, so they keep whatever information is stored inside them, such as the date, camera details and location.

What the developer collects

Nothing. GallerySync has no backend service. The developer does not receive, store, or process your photos, your file names, your email address, your device identifiers, or any usage information. There is no account to register with the developer and no data held about you anywhere.

Third parties

GallerySync communicates with Microsoft — to sign you in, and to reach the OneDrive account you sign in with. That is the only network destination the app uses. Microsoft's handling of the data you store with them is governed by the Microsoft Privacy Statement.

Signing in uses Microsoft's official authentication library. Your password is never seen by GallerySync — you enter it on Microsoft's own sign-in page, and the app only ever receives a token that grants access to your files. GallerySync asks for permission to read and write files in your OneDrive (the Microsoft Files.ReadWrite scope), which is what lets it upload, and bring files back. It does not ask for access to other people's or shared drives.

The app contains no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising SDKs, and no other third-party service that collects or transmits your data. If you have chosen to share usage and diagnostic data with Google on your device, Google Play may give the developer anonymous crash statistics; that is a feature of Android and Google Play, not of GallerySync.

Permissions

  • Photos and videos — to read the media you have chosen to back up. On Android 14 and later you can allow access to only some photos, and the app will tell you when it is working from a partial selection.
  • Folder access you grant through Android's folder picker — when you choose the folders to back up, Android gives GallerySync lasting read and write access to just those folders. Write access is what lets the app replace a photo with a smaller copy, or bring an original back, without asking you every time. It is limited to the folders you chose, and you can revoke it in Android's settings.
  • Internet and network state — to upload to OneDrive, and to wait for a suitable connection before doing so. By default the app uses Wi-Fi only.

The app does not ask for access to your contacts, location, microphone, camera, calendar or messages, and it does not send notifications.

You choose which albums are used, and what happens to each one. Albums you leave switched off are not backed up.

What GallerySync can change

Backing up only adds copies to your OneDrive and touches nothing on your phone. The changes below happen only if you switch them on, and none of them permanently deletes anything.

On your phone

  • Smaller copies (Optimise) — off unless you turn it on. Once the full-size file is confirmed in your OneDrive, the app can replace a photo with a smaller copy (reduced to about 2048 pixels, marked with a small cloud icon) and re-encode a video at a lower resolution. This happens in albums you set to Sync, or, if you choose it during first-time setup, once across the folders you selected there. The full-size original stays in your OneDrive, and the app can bring it back.
  • Archive — only for albums you set to Archive. The app checks that OneDrive holds a file of the same name and size, then asks Android to move your phone's copy to the phone's Trash / Recycle Bin. Android shows its own confirmation. How long the trash keeps files is decided by Android, not by the app. The space is only returned once the trash is emptied, and the app never empties it — you do.
  • Restore. The app can download a full-size original from your OneDrive and put it back on your phone, in place of the smaller copy or in the album it came from.

In your OneDrive

  • The app adds copies of your files, and creates folders to hold them. By default it uses a folder named Samsung Gallery / DCIM, matching the layout Samsung's own sync used; you can choose another. It does not overwrite a file that is already there — a second file with the same name is saved alongside it.
  • By default it never removes anything from OneDrive. There is an optional setting for what to do when you delete a photo from your phone. If you choose to be asked, the app waits (a week, by default), then shows you a list of the OneDrive copies whose phone files are gone, and moves them to your OneDrive recycle bin only after you review the list and confirm. It never empties that recycle bin either.

GallerySync never permanently deletes a file, on your phone or in OneDrive. Anything it removes goes to a trash or recycle bin that you can recover it from, and that only you empty. The copy confirmed in your OneDrive is what protects you; the phone's trash is Android's own behaviour and may differ between phones.

Storage on your device

GallerySync keeps a local database describing your media and its OneDrive copies, and stores your settings and folder choices. This data lives in the app's private storage, is not readable by other apps, and is protected by your device's encryption. The app does not offer your files or this database to other apps.

Android's automatic backup to your Google account may include the app's settings. It does not include your Microsoft sign-in or the file database, which the app excludes on purpose.

Deleting your data

You can sign out at any time, which removes the app's Microsoft sign-in from your device. Uninstalling the app, or clearing its storage in Android's settings, removes the local database and settings. Your files in OneDrive stay where they are — they are yours. Full instructions, including how to revoke the app's access to your Microsoft account, are on the account and data deletion page.

Children

GallerySync is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them. As stated above, the developer collects no information from any user.

Changes to this policy

If the app's handling of data changes, this policy will be updated and the date at the top revised. Material changes will be noted in the app's release notes.
