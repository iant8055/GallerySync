Privacy Policy

GallerySync — last updated 25 September 2026

The short version. GallerySync sends your photos and videos to Cloud storage that you choose and own, and nowhere else. The developer operates no server, receives none of your data, and cannot see your photos, your file names, or your accounts. There is no analytics, no advertising, and no tracking of any kind. GallerySync can also change files on your phone, but only in the ways you choose, and it never permanently deletes anything. See exactly what it can change.

What the app does with your data

GallerySync backs up photos and videos from your device to your own Cloud accounts. To do that it needs to read the media on your device and to sign in to the Cloud services you connect. You can connect any of these:

  • Microsoft OneDrive
  • Google Drive
  • Google Photos (backup only)
  • Dropbox
  • pCloud (backup only)
  • IDrive e2
  • Backblaze B2

You choose which of your folders goes to which Cloud. Nothing is sent anywhere until you have connected a Cloud and chosen folders.

Depending on the choices you make, it can also free up space on your phone once a file is confirmed to be safe in your Cloud, and bring full-size originals back from your Cloud to your phone. Each of these is described under What GallerySync can change below.

Your photos travel from your device directly to the Cloud service you chose, over an encrypted connection. They do not pass through any server belonging to the developer, because no such server exists. Files are uploaded exactly as they are, so they keep whatever information is stored inside them, such as the date, camera details and location.

What the developer collects

Nothing. GallerySync has no backend service. The developer does not receive, store, or process your photos, your file names, your email address, your device identifiers, or any usage information. There is no account to register with the developer and no data held about you anywhere.

Third parties

GallerySync communicates only with the Cloud services you connect, to sign you in and to reach the account you sign in with, and with Google Play for the purchase described below. Each provider's handling of the data you store with them is governed by its own privacy policy: Microsoft, Google, Dropbox, pCloud, IDrive and Backblaze.

For OneDrive, Google Drive, Google Photos, Dropbox and pCloud you sign in on the provider's own page, in your phone's browser. Your password is never seen by GallerySync: the app only ever receives a token that grants the access described below. You can withdraw that access at any time, as described on the account and data deletion page.

  • OneDrive — read and write files (the Microsoft Files.ReadWrite permission). It does not ask for access to other people's or shared drives.
  • Google Drive — the drive.file permission only, which lets the app see and change only the files it created itself. It cannot see the rest of your Drive.
  • Google Photos — permission to add photos and videos, and to read only the ones this app added. It cannot see the rest of your Google Photos library.
  • Dropbox — permission to read and write files.
  • pCloud — the access pCloud grants when you approve the app's sign-in.
  • IDrive e2 and Backblaze B2 — these have no sign-in page. You create an access key in the provider's own console and type it into the app. We recommend a key limited to the one bucket you use for GallerySync.

Purchases. You will receive a 30 Day Free Trial of the GallerySync Pro Version. After your free 30 days you can continue with the Pro Version through a one-time purchase. The purchase is made and processed by Google Play. GallerySync only asks Google Play whether it has been made. Your payment details never reach the app or the developer.

The app contains no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising SDKs, and no other third-party service that collects or transmits your data. If you have chosen to share usage and diagnostic data with Google on your device, Google Play may give the developer anonymous crash statistics; that is a feature of Android and Google Play, not of GallerySync.

Permissions

  • Photos and videos — to read the media you have chosen to back up. On Android 14 and later you can allow access to only some photos, and the app will tell you when it is working from a partial selection.
  • Folder access you grant through Android's folder picker — when you choose the folders to back up, Android gives GallerySync lasting read and write access to just those folders. Write access is what lets the app replace a photo with a smaller copy, or bring an original back, without asking you every time. It is limited to the folders you chose, and you can revoke it in Android's settings.
  • Internet and network state — to upload to your Cloud, and to wait for a suitable connection before doing so. By default the app uses Wi-Fi only.
  • Notifications — used only for the optional Notify when files are ready to archive setting, which is off by default. The app asks for this permission only if you turn that setting on.

The app does not ask for access to your contacts, location, microphone, camera, calendar or messages.

You choose which albums are used, and what happens to each one. Albums you leave switched off are not backed up.

What GallerySync can change

Backing up only adds copies to your Cloud and touches nothing on your phone. The changes below happen only if you switch them on, and none of them permanently deletes anything.

On your phone

  • Smaller copies (Sync) — only for albums you set to Sync. Once the full-size file is confirmed in your Cloud, the app can replace a photo with a smaller copy (reduced to about 2048 pixels, marked with a small cloud icon) and re-encode a video at a lower resolution. The full-size original stays in your Cloud, and the app can bring it back. If you choose it during first-time setup, the same can be done once across the folders you selected there.
  • Archive — only for albums you set to Archive. The app asks your Cloud, at that moment, whether it holds the file at exactly the same size, then asks Android to move your phone's copy to the phone's Trash / Recycle Bin. If the Cloud cannot be asked, nothing is removed. Android shows its own confirmation. How long the trash keeps files is decided by Android, not by the app. The space is only returned once the trash is emptied, and the app never empties it — you do.
  • Restore. The app can download a full-size original from your Cloud and put it back on your phone, in place of the smaller copy or in the album it came from.

Sync, Archive and Restore work with OneDrive, Google Drive, Dropbox, IDrive e2 and Backblaze B2. Google Photos and pCloud are backup only.

In your Cloud

  • The app adds copies of your files, and creates folders to hold them, named for the album they came from. In Google Drive, Dropbox and pCloud these sit inside a folder named GallerySync. In OneDrive the default is a folder named Samsung Gallery / DCIM, matching the layout Samsung's own sync used.
  • The app never removes anything from your Cloud. There is one optional setting, for OneDrive only, for what to do when you delete a photo from your phone. If you choose to be asked, the app shows you a list of the OneDrive copies whose phone files are gone, and moves them to your OneDrive recycle bin only after you review the list and confirm. It never empties that recycle bin either.

GallerySync never permanently deletes a file, on your phone or in any Cloud. Anything it removes goes to a trash or recycle bin that you can recover it from, and that only you empty. The copy confirmed in your Cloud is what protects you; the phone's trash is Android's own behaviour and may differ between phones.

Storage on your device

GallerySync keeps a local database describing your media and its Cloud copies, and stores your settings and folder choices. This data lives in the app's private storage, is not readable by other apps, and is protected by your device's encryption. The app does not offer your files or this database to other apps.

Android's automatic backup to your Google account may include some of the app's settings. It does not include your sign-ins, your keys, or the file database, which the app excludes on purpose.

Deleting your data

You can sign out of any Cloud at any time in Settings. That removes the app's sign-in or keys for that Cloud from your device. If files from that Cloud need restoring, the app first asks whether to leave them there or restore them to your phone. Uninstalling the app, or clearing its storage in Android's settings, removes the local database and settings. Your files in your Cloud stay where they are — they are yours. Full instructions, including how to withdraw the app's access at each provider, are on the account and data deletion page.

Children

GallerySync is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them. As stated above, the developer collects no information from any user.

Changes to this policy

If the app's handling of data changes, this policy will be updated and the date at the top revised. Material changes will be noted in the app's release notes.
